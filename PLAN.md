## Order

1. **`Suitsuki-sashi-shikuchi`** — free and licensed, adds a new `shikuchi`
   family, exercises the angled-seat `approach` path.
2. **台持ち継ぎ（真継ぎ）** — the one splice family on the sheet we lack; the
   plain control next to kanawa and okkake.
3. **雇いホゾ, plain + 三方差し（T字）+ 二方差し（L字）** — one loose-tenon
   family, three configurations; reuses the loose-tenon handling from
   `yatoi-hozo-shachi-sen-shiguchi`.
4. **下小根ホゾ / 上小根ホゾ family** — five labelled variants (差し, 割り楔,
   鼻栓, 打ち抜き込み栓); the densest uncovered cluster. **Done** (second batch).
5. **長ホゾ差し・込み栓打ち**, then **片鎌ホゾ差し** — high-recognition joints,
   cheap to author.
6. **下げ鎌（大入れ）** — a 貫 end with a dropped hook and wedge; needs a lofted
   wedge but no sloped cuts. **Done** (fifth batch).
7. **四方差し** — four beams into one post, staggered through-tenons and plate
   komisen; all box work, but the tenons have to be stacked to avoid collisions.
   **Done** (sixth batch).
8. **傾ぎ大入れ** — a full-section housing with a slanted seam, so the beam's
   lower end runs in under the post's overhanging cheek and cannot lift. Both
   timbers are prisms with a slanted cross-section, which `loft()` already
   provides. **Done** (seventh batch).
9. **追掛け大栓継ぎ（持ち出し継ぎ）** — the half-lap family with the tenons left
   long, so their noses are carried out of the surface; two 大栓 through the
   lap. All box work. **Done** (eighth batch).

Five new assets, taking the collection from 16 to **21**. `suitsuki` was captured with
`tools/sketchfab_capture.mjs` and needed its `assemble` offset corrected from
-155 mm to -200 mm: the generated guess left the beam 45 mm above the post's
shelf, so the tenon never entered its pocket.

The other four have no licensed source model and are authored with
[`tools/author_joint.py`](tools/author_joint.py): exact axis-aligned box CSG on a
coordinate-plane grid, boundary quads merged into rectangles, T-junctions welded.
Their sources are generated in the closed pose and exploded for the animation.
All five were checked for a watertight manifold boundary with consistent winding,
coincident mating faces and zero volumetric overlap between timbers, then baked,
previewed, and `tools/validate.py ../joints` passes.

Five more assets, taking the collection from 21 to **26**. The family needed two
additions to `author_joint.py`: a `loft()` primitive for the tapered wedges and
pegs (two parallel sections joined by planar quads), and `mirror_x()`, because
the site camera looks from +x/+z and the `小根ホゾ` tenon has to face it.

The closed pose is verified by the new
[`tools/check_sources.py`](tools/check_sources.py), which checks the source plus
`assemble` (not just the baked GLBs): every edge used twice in opposite
directions, every face wound with its normal, and no volumetric overlap between
timbers. It probes vertices, edge midpoints and triangle centroids, and also
tests coplanar face pairs, so a 10 mm intrusion is caught in any of x, y or z.
All 26 sources pass.

## Project: the house

The two house references show a two-storey Japanese timber house: `house.jpg`
is a finished cutaway, `house-2.jpg` colour-codes the same frame by
`軸組`, `小屋組` and `床組`. The chosen scope is the **open structural frame**:
no wall or roof finishes, so every connection stays visible, and real joinery
at every structural connection (not a massing model).

### Assumptions

- Footprint **3 bays × 2 bays**, each bay `1.82 m` (one ken), so `5.46 × 3.64 m`
  on plan. This matches the post counts in `house-2.jpg`: four posts across the
  long face, three across the short face.
- Two storeys, `2.70 m` floor-to-floor; `120 mm` posts, sills, beams and
  principal members; `105 mm` studs and braces; `60 mm` joists and rafters.
- `450 mm` foundation above grade, sill top at `570 mm`; a hip roof
  (`寄棟`) with `600 mm` eaves, ridge along the long axis, `隅木` at the four
  hips. Ridge length is `W - D = 1.82 m`, as in the reference.
- Members are named and coloured by the `house-2.jpg` groups: axis (terracotta),
  roof (olive), floor (slate blue), foundation/stone (grey). The palette is
  muted to sit with the existing paper background.
- The house animates as a **construction sequence**, not a closed-joint loop:
  a 27 s `assemble` clip unbuilds it from the ridge down and rebuilds it from
  the foundation up, one timber at a time. Each move is derived from the joint
  that holds the timber, so the frame's connections drive the animation.

The brace, fire-beam, joist and rafter connections are new. Their cut geometry
lives in `tools/author_house.py`, with a short note in `house/README.md`, and a
representative specimen for each now lives in `tools/author_details.py` so every

### Output and size

`house/source.json` uses the same piece/face schema as the joint sources, but
carries group colours and stays in the closed pose; the animation offsets are
derived at export time in `tools/house_anim.py`. `house/house.glb` is written by
the existing GLB writer with one mesh node per timber and flat per-face colours,
no per-face subdivision and no baked AO, so it stays small (2.25 MB with the
clip; aim: under 10 MB).
If it grows past that, the fallback is `.gltf` + external `.bin`, or a
Draco-compressed GLB; the source stays the editable deliverable either way.
The site gets a third tab, **The house**, with one viewer section, and a fourth,
**House details**, with the specimens below; the counts of the existing two tabs
are unchanged.

### Built

The frame is in `house/`: 272 timbers, 15,530 triangles, a 2.25 MB GLB, and a
1100×1100 poster. It has the agreed 3 × 2 ken plan, two storeys, a hip roof,
and real joinery at the connections in the table above. The GLB carries a 27 s
`assemble` clip that takes the frame apart and puts it back together, driven by
the joint each timber is held by: 223 moving timbers in twelve stages, with the
49 foundation and stone pieces static. The original 34-joint catalog is
unchanged, so the two existing tabs and their counts are untouched; the new
house specimens are a fourth tab instead. The third tab is live in `docs/` and
links to [`house/README.md`](house/README.md).

The house tab is now a connection-level viewer rather than a bare
`<model-viewer>`. The GLB is unchanged: `tools/house_links.py` derives
`house/connections.json` from the authored members and joint loops — 494
instances in 27 families, asserted against the frame — and a custom three.js
element (`tools/house_viewer.js`, imported only when the tab opens) raycasts the
timbers, marks their joints, ghosts everything else on a marker hover, and links
each connection to the catalog (exact or related) or to the house schedule.
Click/tap pins a connection, **Browse** is the accessible 494-entry index, and
**Solid / Roof / Upper / Frame** peel the frame back. It starts assembled;
Play/Pause and replay run the same 27 s clip. The 34 joint specimens stay on
`<model-viewer>`, and three.js r160 is vendored (MIT) with its notice under
`tools/vendor/three/`.

## Next

Nothing on either reference sheet is missing, and every `shikuchi` family now
has its own model rather than a near neighbour. The sheet was the right place to
grow first: fifteen of the thirty-four are authored from it, and the imported
set is strong in splices but thinner in column work. The off-sheet tab is now
open for families the sheets do not print, described in the literature rather
than drawn there. Candidates found while researching this batch are the
remaining 蟻継ぎ variants and the other 柱貫 joints (通し貫 and the various
kama forms); each needs a source that fixes the insertion depth and the locking
detail before it is authored.

For any new joint: author or capture the geometry, verify the closed pose, add
`approach` where a seat is sloped or a dovetail has to drop before sliding, then
bake, preview, validate and rebuild `docs/`. Off-sheet work should state which
published description it was drawn from in the credit and `modifications`.

The collision pass added `tools/check_contact.py` as the second closed-pose gate:
`check_sources.py` proves there is no overlap, `check_contact.py` proves each
timber actually touches the frame. Nine joints had 0.4–2.5 mm gaps from
deliberate display clearances or slight misalignment; those are now closed, and
the fire-beam tie gained the `大入れ` housings that the housing manuals call
for. The roof details also use the researched 4寸 birdsmouth proportion (50 mm
seat, 20 mm cheek) instead of the earlier 100 mm seat. Remaining known
approximations are the square-cut hip/jack-rafter ends (real work scribes them
to the side face) and the square kama hook; references and the bearing-only
joints are listed in `joints/README.md`.
