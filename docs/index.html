Daimochi-tsugi#

台持ち継ぎ

2 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Sill, purlin (keta) or beam lengthened directly over a post, where the load arrives in bearing and shear and the splice needs no tension capacity.

Strengths

  • A 50 mm shelf carries the upper timber in bearing on the mortise floor.
  • The housed tenon keeps the two axes aligned without a long scarf.

Watch for

  • Very little pull-out or bending resistance: rely on the support below.
  • The deep housing removes material from the mortised half.
How it goes together

真継ぎ (square butt) with a 50 mm shelf carrying the upper half, a housed tenon resting on the mortise floor, and a komisen through the cheeks.

Doutsuki-ari-tsugi#

胴付き蟻継ぎ

2 beams

2 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Simple dovetail splice cut in plan for a sill or beam that must not pull apart along its length, where a full-depth socket can be cut.

Strengths

  • Flared tenon resists axial withdrawal; the shoulders carry compression.
  • The socket is open top and bottom, so it closes with a straight drop.

Watch for

  • The full-depth socket weakens the receiving member at the seam.
  • The narrow dovetail neck can shear if the joint is driven hard.
How it goes together

A dovetail cut in plan rather than in elevation: the tenon is 60 mm wide at the shoulders and 100 mm at the nose, so once B is lowered over it the two timbers cannot be pulled apart along their length. The socket runs the full 120 mm depth, which is what lets B drop straight down onto the tenon while the shoulders at the seam meet.

Hako-sen-tsugi#

箱栓継ぎ

2 beams

4 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. Low-stakes, short splice in a sill or light beam where only mild pull-resistance is needed and cutting speed matters.

Strengths

  • Box shoulders are quick to cut and give positive alignment.
  • The driven key adds mild resistance to withdrawal.

Watch for

  • Little bending or tension capacity; not for a structural span.
  • A loose key or shrink-fit can let the halves open.

Hira-isuka-tsugi#

平居木継ぎ

2 beams

2 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. Beveled (scissor) scarf placed directly over a post or support, used only for alignment and compression.

Strengths

  • Fastest of the scarves: two matching bevels and no key.
  • Bearing on the support keeps the bevel faces closed.

Watch for

  • No meaningful tension resistance: the bevels can slide apart.
  • Only valid while the splice stays directly over its support.

Kakeawase-komisen-tsugi#

掛け合わせ込み栓継ぎ

2 beams

3 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. General sill or beam splice needing some shear lock plus some pull-resistance, short of a full kanawa-class scarf.

Strengths

  • The hooked shoulder resists shear across the joint.
  • The driven komisen resists lengthwise withdrawal.

Watch for

  • Moderate capacity only: the hook can split a narrow timber.

Kakushi-kanawa-tsugi#

隠し金輪継ぎ

2 beams

4 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. The kanawa structural case - seismic sill or important beam - on a visible face where the wedge pattern must not show.

Strengths

  • Keeps the kanawa-family tension and bending resistance.
  • Locking detail is concealed, so the show face stays clean.

Kanawa-tsugi#

金輪継ぎ

2 beams

3 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. Benchmark strong scarf for seismic sills, foundation footings, wall plates and important beams where real tension and bending resistance are required (JAANUS).

Strengths

  • Oblique housed rabbeted T-shaped tenon plus draw pin resists both tension and bending; two shachi keys may be used instead of the pin.
  • The pin is driven through the central opening to draw the halves tight.

Watch for

  • JAANUS: because tenon and mortise are blind it cannot be slipped together from the side - it must be inserted lengthwise.
  • Complex cut; the key gap and the mortised section must be accurate.

Kata-kama-hozo-sashi#

片鎌ホゾ差し

2 beams

2 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Corner post foot with a one-sided hook, slid along the sill into a slot open at the sill's end; resists uplift in one direction.

Strengths

  • The square back of the hook bears on the sill lip under uplift.
  • Slides in along the sill, so no overhead access is needed.

Watch for

  • Only one direction of pull-out is resisted.
  • The hook needs a slot open at the sill end and sound grain.
How it goes together

The tenon carries a 鎌 hook along one lower edge only. The sill's mortise is a slot open at the sill's end, so the post is slid in along the sill: the hook runs under the sill's lip and the post can no longer be lifted out, while the square face of the hook bears on the lip under uplift. The post's shoulder seats on the sill and its tenon clears the mortise floor.

Katagi-ooire#

傾ぎ大入れ

2 beams

2 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Sloped member - brace or hip rafter - seating into a post or beam at an angle rather than square.

Strengths

  • The whole load bears on the sloped face and the beam cannot lift out.
  • No key is needed; withdrawn straight along its own axis.

Watch for

  • The sloped rebate removes a large part of the post's section.
  • One-directional and hard to adjust once seated.
How it goes together

A full-section housing with the seam cut at a slope rather than square: the beam's end runs 40 mm deeper at the bottom, under the post's overhanging cheek, so the load bears on the slope and the beam cannot be lifted out.

Koshikake-ari-tsugi#

腰掛け蟻継ぎ

2 beams

2 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. The classic sill splice: dovetail resists lengthwise pull, the seat takes vertical load, and the joint drops straight down as sills are laid in sequence.

Strengths

  • Dovetail resists pull; the koshikake seat takes vertical load.
  • Drops straight down, so sills can be laid one after another.

Watch for

  • 下山: without a 竿 the two timbers are hard to keep in a straight line; a 目違い (mechigai) is added when that matters.
  • Plain ari is a weak joint; an over-tight dovetail neck can break.

Naga-hozo-sashi-komisen-uchi#

長ホゾ差し込み栓打ち

2 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Baseline post-to-sill or post-to-beam connection, and the recommended upper-storey post foot where a through tenon can be used.

Strengths

  • Through tenon and side komisen give a reliable, inspectable lock.
  • 下山 recommends a long tenon with a driven pin for post ends.

Watch for

  • The mortise removes a large part of the post's core.
  • The pin hole can split the tenon; the proud nose can catch.
How it goes together

The long tenon passes clean through the post and stands 45 mm proud, so the joint reads as a true through-tenon. A tapered 込み栓 is driven sideways through post and tenon and cut off flush with both cheeks.

Niho-sashi-shikuchi#

二方差し仕口

2 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Corner post receiving beams or nuki from two adjoining faces.

Strengths

  • Simple two-face corner notch; the vertical komisen locks the tongue.
  • Easy to set out at an ordinary building corner.

Watch for

  • The corner notch reduces the post's effective section.
  • The driven pin is visible on the show face.
How it goes together

The post's corner is notched on two adjacent faces (L-shaped in plan); the beam's corner tongue fills it and the komisen is driven down into the post.

Okkake-daisen-tsugi#

追掛け大栓継ぎ

2 beams

4 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. Strong splice that assembles by sliding horizontally - ridge beams already seated on posts, or anywhere a beam cannot be lifted in.

Strengths

  • 下山 calls 追掛け大栓継ぎ きわめて堅固 (extremely firm).
  • Large driven keys give high capacity; slide-in needs no lifting.

Watch for

  • Needs a long lap (8 sun to 1 shaku) and accurate key slots.
  • Requires lateral clearance to slide the lap together.

Okkake-daisen-tsugi (mochidashi)#

追掛け大栓継ぎ（持ち出し継ぎ）

2 beams

4 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. The same high-capacity slide-in splice where the joint must project past its bearing point, for example continuing a beam out over an eave.

Strengths

  • Keeps the okkake-daisen capacity while cantilevering past support.
  • Noses seat in shallow rebates, so the proud ends are controlled.

Watch for

  • Projecting noses need extra rebates and careful setting out.
  • The closed form is an interpretation: the sheet shows only the prepared end, not the assembled joint.
How it goes together

A long half-lap splice in which each tenon runs past the other timber's shoulder: its nose is carried out 20 mm proud of the surface into a shallow rebate, and two 大栓 driven down through the lap pin the two halves together.

Sage-kama-oire#

下げ鎌（大入れ）

2 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. A secondary beam or nuki hung off a primary post: offered up raised, run home, then dropped so the hook bears behind the far face.

Strengths

  • Hook bears on the post's far face and resists pull-out.
  • A wedge over the nose seats it on the mortise floor.

Watch for

  • 下山: wedges in nuki/post joints often loosen under vibration (Kiyomizu-dera's underfloor is inspected for this).
  • The through mortise and hook are awkward to cut.
How it goes together

The nuki's end is cut with a 鎌 hook on its underside. The mortise is one hook deeper than the nuki, so the nuki is offered up raised, run home and then dropped: the hook falls behind the post's far face and bears on it, so the nuki cannot be pulled back out. A wedge driven in over the nose seats the nuki on the mortise floor and keeps the hook tight. The nuki is housed at its full section with no shoulder — 大入れ — and the mortise runs right through the post, leaving a narrow cheek either side.

Sampo-sashi-shachi-sen#

三方差し車知栓

4 beams

7 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Three-direction T-junction post with beams arriving from three directions, each locked by a tapered 車知栓 - built like the 四方差し figure in 建築用語集 but with one arrival omitted.

Strengths

  • A shachi-sen through each tenon resists withdrawal on all three arrivals without a separate key per face.
  • Tenons sit at stacked levels, so none share the post's core.

Watch for

  • Through tenons leave a large lost section in the post (the same warning 建築用語集 gives 四方差し).
  • Interpreted from the 四方差し figure with one arrival removed; it is not a separately sourced named specimen.
How it goes together

Three beams meet one post at a T-junction. Each tenon sits at its own level, runs 100 mm into the post and is locked by a tapered 車知栓 driven through it from the beam's side; the pair of keys on opposite faces draws the arrivals tight.

Sampo-sashi-shikuchi#

三方差し仕口

2 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Post at a T-junction, for example an interior wall meeting an exterior wall line, with beams arriving from three directions.

Strengths

  • Mortise opens on three faces, so the beam tenon drops straight in.
  • A vertical komisen through beam, tenon and post locks the lot.

Watch for

  • The open-top housing weakens the post head.
  • Only moderate pull resistance; the pin must be driven down.
How it goes together

The post's end is housed through its width, so the mortise opens on the top and both sides (three faces); the beam's tenon drops in and the komisen is driven down through beam, tenon and post.

四方蟻継ぎ

2 beams

2 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. Four-way dovetail interlock for a junction where all four directions need pull-out resistance, not just friction or a pin.

Strengths

Shihou-sashi-shachi-sen#

四方差し車知栓

5 beams

9 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Four-way post connection locked with paired 車知栓 wedge keys - the configuration shown in 建築用語集's 四方差し figure, where the red member is the shachi-sen.

Strengths

  • Paired wedge keys draw all four tenons tight and resist withdrawal.
  • Opposite tenons pass through the post and tie one another.

Watch for

  • Same warning as 四方差し: passing tenons leave a large lost post section; metal reinforcement may be needed.
  • Four tenons and two keys must be set out at distinct levels.
How it goes together

Four beams frame into one post. The two x tenons stack below the two z tenons so none share the post's core, and a tapered 車知栓 through each tenon locks it. 建築用語集 shows the 車知栓 as the red member in its 四方差し figure, while warning that the through tenons leave a large lost section in the post.

Shihou-sashi-shikuchi#

四方差し

5 beams

7 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Free-standing interior post or central column with beams or nuki converging from all four sides at one level.

Strengths

  • Opposite tenons pass through the post and 30 mm into the beam opposite, so the four beams tie one another.
  • Tenons are stacked at different levels instead of sharing one core.

Watch for

  • 建築用語集: tenons through the post leave a large lost section; neat in appearance but not necessarily strong - consider metal reinforcement.
  • Two plate komisen must be dropped accurately and trimmed flush.
How it goes together

Four 床梁 frame into one post at a single level. Two through-tenons cannot share a core, so each beam's 竿 is cut at its own level within the beam's depth: the two x beams stack in the lower half, the two z beams in the upper half. Each tenon passes clean through the post and 30 mm into the end of the beam opposite, so the four beams tie one another, and a plate komisen dropped through each pair of tenons from the post's top face, standing a little proud, pins the lot.

Shitakone-hozo-tasuki-gake#

下小根ホゾ（襷掛付）

2 beams

2 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Blind lower-tier tenon at a corner where a vertical 襷 bridge in the post must also tie the arriving beams together.

Strengths

  • Forked small root straddles the 襷 bridge, resisting sideways pull.
  • Blind mortise leaves the post's far face clean.

Watch for

  • The bridge and twin pockets are intricate to lay out.
  • Less accessible for repair than a through joint.
How it goes together

The tenon is blind, so nothing shows on the post's far face. Its small root is forked into two prongs and the post keeps a vertical 襷 bridge between the two mortise pockets, so the joint resists sideways pull as well as withdrawal.

Tsutsumikomi-nuki#

包み込み貫

2 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Nuki wrapped by the post: a shelf under its rebated end and a wall beyond it, used where a nuki must not pull out.

Strengths

  • The end is caught between shelf and wall rather than merely housed.
  • A wedge in the mouth gap holds it down.

Watch for

  • Three-stage assembly (raise, slide, lower) needs access.
  • A 90 mm insertion removes much of the post's section.
How it goes together

The nuki's end is wrapped by the post. It runs 90 mm into the 120 mm post and its last 15 mm is rebated 15 mm up from the underside; the post keeps a shelf under that rebate and a 30 mm wall beyond the end. Offer the nuki up 15 mm, run it home over the shelf, then lower it onto the shelf so the end is caught, and drive the wedge into the gap above its back.

Use. Upper-tier through tenon where the member above the post needs the fuller bearing shoulder and the pin passes through both cheeks.

Strengths

Watari-ago-niju-hozo-shikuchi#

渡り顎二重枘仕口

3 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Double-tenon crossing over a post: a 桁 girder sits on the post's lower tenon, a 梁 beam crosses on it, the post's upper tenon runs through girder and beam alike and is cut flush with the beam's top, and a 渡り顎 housing in the girder takes the beam's chin at the crossing.

Strengths

  • The lower tenon fixes the girder on the post without hollowing the post's core.
  • The upper tenon runs right through the girder and the beam, so the joint resists uplift and ties all three timbers in line.
  • The 渡り顎 housing and chin stop the beam shifting along the crossing, and the shoulders either side keep the beam's bearing full width.

Watch for

  • The girder loses its housing depth (about 18 mm) across a 78 mm band, and the beam loses the same at its chin.
  • The through tenon and the jaw must be set out together; the carpentry-school build log records over two hours of scribing and a struggle to open the mortise right through.
How it goes together

A post carries two stacked tenons: the lower one takes the 桁 girder, the upper one runs through both the girder and the 梁 beam above and is cut flush with the beam's top, and a 渡り顎 housing across the girder takes the beam's chin. The layout follows the carpentry-school build log (hainankenchiku.jimdofree.com; diyshufu.com/job-training-of-carpenter/1754/) and the assembly GIF by @thejoinery_jp (tweet 771307153583812610).

Yatoi-hozo-shachi-sen-shiguchi#

雇い枘車知栓仕口

2 beams

5 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY-SA 4.0, modified. Shared alike under the same license. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. Repair or retrofit where both receiving timbers are already fixed and the connection is structurally critical.

Strengths

  • Separate tenon plus paired wedge keys gives a strong, adjustable lock.
  • Neither existing member has to supply the tenon.

Watch for

  • Loose tenon and two keys need very accurate matching mortises.
  • Keys can loosen; access to both faces is needed.

Yatoi-hozo-shikuchi#

雇い枘仕口

2 beams

3 pieces · model by Japanese joinery project, CC BY 4.0, modified. Source in this repository.

Use. Light-duty connection where both receiving members are already fixed in place and neither can supply a tenon.

Strengths

  • A separate loose tenon seats in the post and the beam slides over it.
  • The tenon can be replaced without disturbing either timber.

Watch for

  • Not cut from either member, so capacity is lower than an integral tenon.
  • Blind mortises must line up exactly.
How it goes together

The loose tenon stays seated in the post; the beam's blind mortise slides over it, so the tenon is revealed when the beam is withdrawn.

Yose-ari-hozo-shikuchi#

寄せ蟻枘仕口

2 beams

2 pieces · model by thejoinery, CC BY 4.0, modified. Original on Sketchfab.

Use. Mid-span joist connection assembled by sliding horizontally, where the dovetail draws the joint snug as it is driven.

Strengths

  • The sloping dovetail pulls the members tight on entry.
  • Good where a drop-in joint is not possible.

Watch for

  • The dovetail neck can shear if overdriven (蟻首がとぶ).
  • Needs clear access along the slide direction.

About

Models by thejoinery (CC BY 4.0, one CC BY-SA 4.0), read from the public Sketchfab viewer, split into timbers, re-lit with a baked paper-sunrise look and given an assembly animation. The animations were checked for timbers passing through each other, and some meshes were corrected where the author's pose was not the true closed joint.

20 of the specimens have no licensed source model: they are authored for this collection from the reference sheets and published descriptions, and released CC BY 4.0. Per-joint notes and references are in joints/README.md.

The beams count is the number of timber pieces meeting at the joint: the post plus the beams, girders, joists, rafters or braces; pegs, wedges, keys, dowels and loose tenons are not counted. Counts in this collection: 2: 33, 3: 1, 4: 1, 5: 2. The strength and weakness notes are compiled in tools/joint_meta.py from the naming guide, the reference sheets and the sources linked below.
