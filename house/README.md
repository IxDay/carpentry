# The house

The open structural frame of the two-storey house in [`house.jpg`](../house.jpg)
and [`house-2.jpg`](../house-2.jpg). It is a **frame**, not a finished building:
there are no walls, floor boards, roof boards or tiles, so every post, beam and
joint stays visible.

- `source.json` — the frame, in the same face schema as the joint sources. One
  list with one level, `house`, and 272 timber pieces. The animation offsets are
  derived at export time, so the source stays the closed pose.
- `house.glb` — one mesh node per timber, flat per-face colours, 15,530
  triangles, about 2.25 MB. The rest pose is assembled; the `assemble` clip
  unbuilds and rebuilds the frame in 27 seconds.
- `connections.json` — 272 pieces and 494 connection instances in 27 families,
  with the members, point and marker anchor of every joint. Generated by
  [`tools/house_links.py`](../tools/house_links.py); see below.
- `sunset_house.png` — the poster render of the shipped GLB at rest.

## Plan and dimensions

| | |
|---|---|
| plan | 3 bays × 2 bays, one ken (`1.82 m`) each → **5.46 × 3.64 m** |
| grid | four post lines across the long face, three across the short face |
| storeys | two, `2.70 m` floor-to-floor |
| principal members | `120 mm` posts, sills, plates and beams |
| studs, braces | `105 mm` and `90 mm` |
| joists, rafters | `60 mm` and `45 × 90 mm` |
| foundation | `450 mm` strip and pad footings, sill top at `570 mm` |
| roof | hip (`寄棟`), ridge along the long axis, `600 mm` eaves, ridge `1.82 m` long |

The ridge is deliberately only `W - D = 1.82 m` long, as in the reference: the
four hips meet it at the ends.

Coordinates are metres, Y up, in the closed pose. The origin is the centre of
the ground plan at grade. The frame is built in three layers:

- **base** — plinth, strip and pad footings, foundation stones, floor stones;
- **floor** — 床束, 大引 and 根太 on the ground floor, six upper 根太;
- **axis** — sills, posts, studs, 貫, braces, 火打梁, 胴差, 床梁 and 軒桁;
- **roof** — 小屋梁, 小屋束, 真束, 母屋, 棟木, 隅木, 垂木 and the jack rafters.

The colours follow the groups in `house-2.jpg`: axis terracotta, roof olive,
floor slate blue, foundation and stone grey. They are muted to sit with the
paper background. Lighting is the same baked directional term as the joint set
and the GLB is written with `KHR_materials_unlit`, so a viewer should not add
its own lights.

## Unbuild and rebuild

The clip is a construction sequence, not a single exploded pose. Each of the
223 moving timbers is held by its own connection, so `tools/house_anim.py`
derives its track from that joint: a post lifts off its sill tenon, a 貫 slides
out of its through mortise, a brace withdraws square to the wall, a rafter lifts
out of its birdsmouths. Only after the short extraction does the member fly to a
parked pose above and slightly outside the frame. The 49 foundation and stone
pieces never move.

The house comes apart in reverse construction order - rafters and hips, ridge,
purlins, roof posts and roof beams, wall plates, upper walls, second-floor
beams, lower walls, then floor framing - and goes back together in the reverse.
Stages overlap, with a small deterministic stagger inside each stage so a wall
does not move as one rigid sheet. The midpoint is the bare foundation. The clip
starts and ends assembled, so it can loop or be replayed without a jump.

The connection schedule below is what the motions are derived from.

## Connection metadata and the interactive viewer

`connections.json` is built from the same member IDs and joint loops as
`author_house.py`: every connection names the timbers it joins, a point in the
assembled pose, and the member whose motion its marker follows. The tool
asserts every id against the authored frame, so a renamed or removed timber
fails the build instead of leaving a marker in mid-air. The points are placed
deliberately, not guessed from bounding boxes: a 貫-to-stud slot is recorded at
the stud, a hip-to-plate crossing at the plate, a floor post on its stone.

The house tab loads the frame with a small custom element,
[`tools/house_viewer.js`](../tools/house_viewer.js), and only imports it when
the tab is first opened. The 34 joint specimens stay on `<model-viewer>`. The
frame starts assembled; **Play / Pause** (or Space) runs the same 27 s clip and
**R** replays it from the start. Hovering a timber outlines it and marks its
connections. Hovering a marker ghosts every other timber, names the joint,
describes it and links to the matching catalog specimen — *exact* where the
house detail is the same, *related* where the catalog shows the family in
another configuration — or to the schedule below when the detail is house-only.
Click or tap a timber or marker to pin it; Escape or **Clear focus** releases
it. **Browse** is the full connection index: 494 entries grouped by family,
with a filter, and selecting one focuses it in the scene. **Solid / Roof /
Upper / Frame** peel the frame back without moving the camera: hide the roof,
the upper storey, or the masonry. A WebGL or fetch failure falls back to the
poster and a GLB download rather than an empty stage.

`tools/build_site.py` copies the viewer, the metadata and a pinned three.js
r160 under `docs/`; see [`tools/vendor/README.md`](../tools/vendor/README.md).

## Joint schedule

| Connection | Joint in the model |
|---|---|
| post ↔ sill | through tenon, protruding below the sill with a wedge |
| post ↔ 胴差 / 軒桁 | housed tenon in the post, 55 mm deep, with a komisen peg through the post |
| beam ↔ post | the post is continuous; each bay's beam has a housed tenon at both ends |
| 貫 ↔ post | 包み込み貫, scaled: a `105 × 120 mm` nuki, a 15 mm housing in the post, and a wedge on the outer face |
| 筋違 ↔ post / sill / beam | brace let into the post faces; its thickness is the housing |
| 火打梁 ↔ 土台 / 胴差 | diagonal corner tie resting on the sill or girder |
| 根太 ↔ 大引 | half-lap notch, 30 mm out of each member |
| 根太 ↔ 土台 | 30 mm housed end |
| 床束 ↔ 大引 | tenon into the sleeper, on a stone |
| 小屋梁 ↔ 軒桁 | housed tenon into the inner face of the plate |
| 小屋束 ↔ 小屋梁 | tenon into the top of the beam |
| 母屋 ↔ 小屋束 | the post runs 40 mm into the purlin; the purlin is notched over it |
| 垂木 ↔ 母屋 / 軒桁 / 棟木 | birdsmouth notch at each crossing |
| 隅木 ↔ 棟木 | the hip butts the ridge end; the ridge is notched for the 真束 |
| 土台 corners | half-lapped, alternating by wall |
| 胴差 / 軒桁 at posts | housed tenon, as at the other beam ends |

The brace, fire-beam, joist and rafter connections do not appear on the joint
reference sheets. They are authored in [`tools/author_house.py`](../tools/author_house.py)
and are **not** part of the 34-joint catalog: the house is one model, not a
joint specimen, and the site's joint counts do not change. If one of these
connections is wanted as a specimen later, its cut geometry can be lifted into
`tools/author_joint.py` and the catalog.

## Building

```sh
python3 tools/author_house.py      # house/source.json
python3 tools/export_house.py      # house/house.glb, with the clip
python3 tools/house_links.py       # house/connections.json
python3 tools/preview.py house/source.json --sunset --level house \
    --size 1100 --ss 1 --glbdir house --outdir house \
    --margin 0.92 --bottom 0.01 --sun-yaw 40 --sun-pitch 27
python3 tools/build_site.py        # adds the third tab to docs/
```

`tools/house_geom.py` holds the dimensions, palette and the members built in a
local frame (braces, hips, rafters). The rest is axis-aligned box CSG via
`author_joint.compile_solid`, so the cuts are exact rectangles and the source
stays editable. `tools/house_anim.py` derives the per-timber tracks from the
joint type and the construction stage; `tools/export_house.py` writes them with
the meshes.

## What is left out

- wall and roof finishes, floor and roof boards, tiles and metalwork;
- the second-floor deck boards: the upper 根太 are shown, but the deck is left
  open so the ground-floor frame stays visible;
- door and window openings, stairs, and the interior partitions;
- the exact roof profile at the hips (the hips and jack rafters are cut square,
  not scribed), and the curved or tapered ends some of the reference timbers
  have.
